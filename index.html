Natal positions from your almanac

This app places the Sun, the Moon and the nodes to about an arcminute, but the five true planets come from Keplerian elements without perturbation terms — good to six to sixteen arcminutes, which is enough to move a pada or a navamsa. A full ephemeris is far beyond what fits in one offline file.

So paste your almanac's own table instead. AstroSage's planet list copies straight in; Jagannatha Hora and printed panchangs work too. The natal chart is then built from figures you trust, and the dasha follows the Moon in it.

Getting the figures off a screenshot. This app has no text recognition — an offline OCR engine needs megabytes of trained data and would dwarf the app itself. Your phone already has one, though: open the AstroSage screenshot in Google Photos or the screenshot preview, tap Select text (or Google Lens), copy the table, and paste it under Paste below. The reader copes with what that produces — missing degree symbols, merged columns, O read as zero.

Pick the rashi, then type the degree however it appears — 02°09'28", 2 9 28 and 2:9:28 all read the same. Leave a row blank to keep this app's own figure for that graha.

Calibrate the dasha to your almanac

Vimshottari hangs entirely off one number: how far the Moon had crossed its nakshatra at birth. On a 20-year Venus nakshatra, one arcsecond of Moon moves every boundary by 3.7 hours — identically at all five levels. That is why the Mahadasha can look right while the Sookshma is days out, and why no two programs agree down at Prana.

Read the natal Moon off AstroSage or Jagannatha Hora and type it here. The whole chain is then built from your reference instead of from this app's ephemeris, and both halves follow it. If you pasted a full table above, this is already filled in from its Moon — there is one Moon, not two.

A reference program's displayed Moon and its own dasha table can differ by a few arcseconds, which is hours of chain. If a boundary you trust is still off after calibrating, slide everything here — positive moves the sequence later. Programs disagree about this, and the disagreement is the usual reason two apps give different Pratyantar and Sookshma dates. The gap between the first two options is 0.0075 days a year — invisible in a Mahadasha, and larger than a whole Prana period once you are decades into the cycle. Set it to match whichever almanac you check against; both the chart and the running-period readout follow it.

Accuracy

Moon: Meeus ch.47, full 60-term series — matches the published reference to 0.13″. Sun: Meeus ch.25, ~11″. Planets: JPL Keplerian elements, ~1–2′ for 1800–2050. Ayanamsa: Lahiri (Chitrapaksha), 23°51′11″ at J2000. Tithi, nakshatra and yoga boundaries land within about a minute of clock time.

Conventions: true node · Amanta month naming · day runs sunrise to sunrise · Rahu Kaal as eighths of daylight · 24 equal horas in Chaldean order.
